Trees are vital, long-lived woody plants that serve as the backbone of our planet's ecosystems. By using their leaves to convert sunlight into food through photosynthesis, they act as natural air purifiers, absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing the essential oxygen we breathe. Their complex structures—comprising deep root networks, sturdy trunks, and expansive leafy crowns—provide critical habitats for millions of species, stabilize the soil against erosion, and help regulate global temperatures. From towering coastal redwoods to resilient desert varieties, these remarkable organisms are fundamental to sustaining life on Earth.
